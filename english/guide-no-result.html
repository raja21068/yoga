Do Not Seek Results

When practice keeps asking, 'What will I get from this?' meditation becomes another transaction of the mind.

Read the Book3. Guidelines to Freedom

What this chapter is saying

The book says the ego is naturally oriented toward results and loves shortcuts. It can turn meditation into a project: how long to enlightenment, how many days to results, whether this session produced a special experience.

The direction in the book

Meditation is described here as a state without goal-orientation. This does not mean life can have no goals; it means that while meditating, this moment is not merely a tool for obtaining a reward in the future.

What it means for practice

The three-day experiment is not a promise that 'three days will work.' It simply gives a method enough time to be genuinely experienced. At the end, a more useful question than 'What did I get?' is: was I really inside this practice while it was happening?

After Practice
  1. Do not begin by giving the session an effectiveness score.
  2. Feel what the body, breath, mind, and emotions are like now.
  3. If a result comes, let it reveal itself; do not chase it.
