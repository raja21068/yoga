Keep It as Simple as Possible

Witnessing begins with the most concrete—the body—then deepens into thought, feeling, mood, and finally the witness itself. There is no need to make it complicated.

Read the Book10. Keep It as Simple as Possible

What is the question?

The questioner is confused about how to distinguish one part of the mind observing another part from true witnessing, and asks whether the witness can witness itself.

The sequence given in the book

Begin with what is fixed and easy: walking, sitting, sleeping, eating. Then watch thoughts, then feelings, then subtler moods. As witnessing strengthens, it becomes like a candle at night that lights not only the surroundings but itself.

Avoid a new achievement identity

The book also warns that the moment you conclude, 'I have done it; I have become the witness,' you have made another identity. Witnessing is an open-ended deepening process. Religion need not become thousands of rules; find one principle that is harmonious with you and live it fully.

Begin with the ABCs
  1. First witness one simple bodily movement, such as raising a hand or walking ten steps.
  2. Once that is steady, watch thoughts; do not begin with the most abstract question of 'who is witnessing whom?'
  3. If 'I finally did it' appears, include that sentence within what is being witnessed.
