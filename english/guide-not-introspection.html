Meditation Is Not Introspection

Introspection analyzes 'why am I angry?' Witnessing directly sees the anger itself—without explanation, judgment, or a hurry to change it.

Read the Book2. Wrong Methods

What this chapter is saying

The book separates introspection from direct awareness. Introspection studies causes, judges good and bad, and plans not to repeat the mistake; all of this is still thinking. Witnessing is concerned with this moment and directly meets the feeling that is actually present.

The three stages in the book

Using anger as an example, the book says awareness gradually arrives earlier. At first you see only the 'tail' after anger has passed; later you can see the whole 'elephant' at the height of anger; later still, awareness is present as anger is just entering consciousness.

What it means for practice

You do not have to turn every emotion into a psychological investigation. Train direct seeing first: where is the body tight, hot, fast? How does thought feed the emotion? How does the feeling rise? Clear seeing itself changes your relationship to emotion.

Do Not Rush to Explain
  1. The next time emotion rises, do not begin by asking 'why?'
  2. Simply note what is happening now: bodily sensation, breath, thought speed, impulse.
  3. After the emotion passes, do not blame yourself; notice whether next time you can see its beginning a little earlier.
