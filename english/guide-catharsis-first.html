Begin with Catharsis

When much has accumulated inside, the book suggests beginning with movement, expression, and catharsis before entering silence.

Read the Book2. Advice for Beginners

What this chapter is saying

For some beginners, trying to 'just sit' only magnifies repression and mental chaos. The book therefore recommends giving accumulated anger, tears, laughter, movement, and energy a safe outlet first.

The contrast in the book

Beginning with wild movement can make an inner silent point easier to notice; forcing the outside to be still can leave only suppressed madness within. The book uses this contrast to explain why active meditation often prepares the way for passive meditation.

What it means for practice

Catharsis does not mean dumping emotion on other people or becoming destructive. The book explicitly says not to interfere with others and not to destroy. Expression is meant to create an outlet for what has accumulated, making room for genuine quiet.

Safety Principle
  1. Choose a clearly structured active meditation rather than exploding at random.
  2. Give yourself enough space to avoid hurting yourself, others, or objects.
  3. After catharsis, always leave a quiet stage so witnessing can follow.
