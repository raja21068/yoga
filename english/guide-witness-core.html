Witnessing: The Soul of Meditation

The core of meditation is not creating a special experience, but remaining aware within action, thought, and feeling without losing the center.

Read the Book1. What Is Meditation?

What this chapter is saying

The book first moves meditation away from 'doing something' and back toward simply being. At first, taste brief moments of quiet being. Then, even while cleaning, bathing, walking, or working, learn not to lose your center. Action continues, but witnessing is not carried away by action.

The example in the book

The book tells the story of a watchman: one man watches the outer gate; another turns the direction back and 'watches the watcher.' This change of direction moves from endless pursuit of objects toward awareness itself.

What it means for practice

Witnessing does not depend on a special object. A tree, river, birdsong, body, thought, even the noise of the mind can be seen. The key is not what is seen but whether awareness is present in seeing. The book unfolds awareness through body, thought, feeling/mood, and finally awareness of awareness itself.

Try This Today
  1. Choose an ordinary action such as walking, washing your hands, or drinking tea.
  2. Do not slow the action down to create a 'meditative feeling'; simply know that it is happening while it happens.
  3. If thought carries you away, noticing that fact is already a return.
