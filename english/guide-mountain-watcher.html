The Watcher on the Hill

When you feel that you are neither fully in the world nor yet the 'watcher on the hill,' the book's answer is surprising: that is exactly where you should be.

Read the Book3. The Watcher on the Hill

What is the question?

The questioner imagines two ideal positions: either be totally in worldly life or become the detached watcher on the hill. Being in between feels like a mistake.

The turn in the answer

The book points to the word 'should.' The moment there is 'I should be elsewhere, I should become someone else,' desire and rejection begin. It emphasizes accepting your present position. Heaven is not waiting at some future summit; acceptance changes the quality of where you already are.

What it means for practice

Witnessing does not require manufacturing an ideal version of yourself. First stop fighting your present position: less becoming, more being. The summit is not merely a future destination; it appears as inner space when resistance relaxes.

First Stop Trying to Move Elsewhere
  1. When 'I should be calmer / more spiritual / more aware' appears, first see the 'should.'
  2. Name your actual position now: confused, clear, happy, tired—this is where practice begins.
  3. Say one complete 'yes' to this moment and notice whether the quality of seeing changes.
