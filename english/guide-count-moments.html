Count the Moments of Awareness

When you forget awareness, do not create guilt. The book shifts the emphasis from 'I failed again' to 'I have just awakened once more.'

Read the Book9. Count the Moments of Awareness

What is the question?

The questioner tries to remain aware at work but often forgets. Whenever he notices that he was not aware, he feels guilty, as if he has made a mistake.

The turn in the answer

The book suggests starting with simple activities such as walking and eating instead of demanding, from the beginning, that you be totally involved and totally aware during complex work. Awareness first needs to become as natural as breathing.

Recalculate

When you notice 'I forgot just now,' awareness has already returned in that very moment. Do not count every failure; count these moments of waking up. Guilt makes practice heavy; appreciating the moments of awakening lets them gradually lengthen and the gaps between them shorten.

Today, Count Only the Moments of Waking Up
  1. Begin with a simple activity such as walking or eating.
  2. When you forget, do not blame yourself. When you remember, lightly mark one moment of 'waking up.'
  3. At the end of the day, remember a few moments when you were truly clear instead of counting how many times you failed.
