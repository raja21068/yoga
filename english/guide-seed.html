Witnessing Is Like Planting a Seed

Witnessing is the beginning; no-mind is the flowering. You can plant the seed, but spring cannot be forced by will.

Read the Book11. Witnessing Is Like Planting a Seed

What is the question?

The questioner can increasingly witness body, thoughts, and feelings, but moments of no-mind still feel rare and distant. So he asks how witnessing leads to no-mind.

The metaphor in the book

The book compares witnessing to a seed and no-mind to the final flowering. Planting is easy; then come waiting, conditions, and patience. You can prepare the soil, but you cannot pull the petals open with your hands.

How the process happens

Thoughts receive energy from identification. When you can say, 'I see anger appearing,' instead of becoming completely angry, distance has appeared between you and the thought. The greater the distance, the less energy the thought receives, and gaps of no-mind can gradually expand from occasional seconds.

Do Not Dig Up the Seed to Check It
  1. Continue witnessing the body, thoughts, and feelings you are already able to see.
  2. Be grateful for brief moments of no-mind, but do not chase or reproduce them.
  3. Use the measure offered in the book: are you becoming more joyful, more sensitive, and more able to witness?
