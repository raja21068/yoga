Witnessing Is Enough

Faced with millions of years of conditioning, 'just witness' can sound too simple. The book nevertheless brings all methods back to this center.

Read the Book12. Witnessing Is Enough

What is the question?

The questioner asks: if the mind is so stubborn, don't we need to do something besides witnessing in order to tame it?

The lamp in the book

The book uses the image of a house with the light on: thieves do not enter when the light is burning; when light appears, darkness does not need to be separately driven away. In the same way, when witnessing is present, the mind's old mechanical activity loses its dominance.

How the book comes full circle

As witnessing matures, the mind is not destroyed; it returns to the position of servant, becoming a tool when needed and resting when not needed. The book finally says that the one hundred and twelve methods differ in form, but their center is the art of witnessing.

Do One Thing Less: Do Not Start a Second War with the Mind
  1. When a thought appears, first witness it; do not immediately add a second activity—'How should I destroy this thought?'
  2. Use the mind when it is needed; when it is not needed, let it rest.
  3. If things become complicated, return to the simplest point: is what is happening now being clearly seen?
