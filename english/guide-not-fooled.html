Do Not Be Deceived by Experiences

Anything that can be experienced—pain, pleasure, silence, energy, emptiness—is still content within consciousness. The book turns attention back toward the experiencer.

Read the Book3. The Tricks of the Mind

What this chapter is saying

The book warns seekers not to mistake unusual experiences for the destination. Experiences change: joy today, emptiness tomorrow, silence the next day. If it can be seen by you, it is still content within awareness.

The real question

Spiritual seeking shifts here from 'What happened?' to 'To whom is all this happening?' Experiences may be beautiful or intense, but the experiencer—the awareness that can see all these changes—is where the book continually points.

What it means for practice

After meditation, light, vibration, energy, silence, or strong happiness may appear. There is no need to deny them and no need to hold them. Enjoy their passing while continuing to witness: this experience too is being seen. Do not build a new identity from one beautiful event.

Experiences Can Come and Go
  1. After practice, describe the facts before concluding 'I have reached such-and-such a state.'
  2. Whenever an experience appears, add one reminder: this too is being seen.
  3. Gently move interest from the content of experience back toward the space that is aware of it.
