Enjoy Unawareness Too

Awareness has peaks and valleys; forgetting does not automatically mean failure.

Read the Book3. Guidelines to Freedom

What this chapter is saying

The book treats unawareness as a possible kind of rest, like waking in the day and sleeping at night. If 'I must be aware twenty-four hours a day' becomes a tense demand, awareness itself becomes a burden.

The image in the book

Awareness and unawareness are compared to peaks and valleys; happiness and sadness also change in this way. The key is that when you can see both, you begin to discover that you are not identical with either.

What it means for practice

After getting lost in thought, there is no need to blame yourself. In the moment you notice 'I was unaware just now,' awareness has already returned. Let returning be part of the practice.

When the Mind Wanders
  1. Do not compensate, get angry, or try to erase what happened.
  2. Simply acknowledge: I forgot; now I see.
