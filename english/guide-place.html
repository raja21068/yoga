The Right Place

In the beginning, environment can support you. As meditation stabilizes, it becomes less dependent on environment.

Read the Book2. Advice for Beginners

What this chapter is saying

The book initially recommends natural, quiet, relatively undisturbed places. If that is not possible, use a small corner at home that is reserved for meditation. Returning to the same place creates familiarity and a cue for entering the practice.

The image in the book

The beginner is compared to a young tree that has not yet taken root and needs some protection. Once the roots are strong, wind and rain can make the tree stronger. Likewise, later on, stations, travel, and noise need not be decisive obstacles.

What it means for practice

Do not turn 'I need the perfect environment' into an excuse to postpone practice. In the beginning, create support where you can; in the long run, let awareness become less dependent on place.

Prepare a Corner
  1. Choose a place that is easy to use repeatedly.
  2. Keep it simple; it does not need to be filled with religious objects.
  3. Let the place have one clear function: to remind you to enter meditation.
