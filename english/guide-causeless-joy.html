A Lasting Joy Without Cause

The book distinguishes happiness triggered by outer conditions from an inner joy that does not depend on a special reason.

Read the Book2. Meditation in Flowering

What this chapter is saying

Getting what you want, meeting someone you like, or having circumstances go your way can bring happiness. The book reminds us that this kind of happiness changes when conditions change. Another kind of joy cannot easily answer the question 'why?' because it is not caused by a particular object.

The key distinction in the book

Outer conditions continually change: youth becomes old age, favorable circumstances turn, possessions can be lost. The book points toward finding an inner source that is not completely dependent on those changes.

What it means for practice

This is not a demand to be happy all the time or a denial of sadness. It simply shifts attention from 'something happened, therefore I am happy' toward 'when nothing special is happening, can I also sense ease or contentment in simply being?'

Look at It This Way Today
  1. When happiness appears, first know that it is there.
  2. If it clearly comes from a condition, do not deny it; simply notice the relationship.
  3. Sometimes, in a moment when nothing special is happening, see whether there is also a little ease that does not need a reason.
