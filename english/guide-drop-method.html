When to Drop the Method

A method is a boat for crossing the river: use it when needed, and once you have truly reached the other shore, do not carry the boat on your head.

Read the Book1. Methods and Meditation

What this chapter is saying

The book specifically warns against attachment to methods. A method may have helped you, but that does not mean it must be repeated forever. When awareness is happening clearly by itself, holding onto the method can become a burden.

The story in the book

Buddha's image is of people who cross a river and, out of gratitude, carry the boat on their heads as they continue walking. The problem is not the boat; the problem is not knowing when to put it down.

What it means for practice

At first, clarity may come only in flashes and the mind returns. The book does not say, 'Once you have seen it, stop forever.' It says: when awareness is clear, drop the method in that moment; when it is lost, you can use the method again as a bridge.

Recognize the Moment
  1. If you still need the method to become steady, keep using it.
  2. If awareness is standing on its own in a particular moment, do not keep manufacturing technique in that moment.
  3. Do not drop a method too early because you think you 'should have advanced.'
