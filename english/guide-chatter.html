Part Four · Obstacles to Meditation · 1. The Two Difficulties

The Chattering Mind

The second great difficulty is not simply that thoughts exist, but that the mind instantly translates every experience into language, putting words between existence and awareness.

Read the Book1. The Two Difficulties

What this chapter is saying

The book calls continuous inner verbalizing the second major obstacle. You see a flower, a sunrise, or a person and the mind immediately names, comments, and compares; before the experience is directly touched, it has already become words and memory.

The key in the book

The point is not to use one part of the mind to suppress another. The book repeatedly says not to fight the mind. Watch thoughts like clouds, let them come and go, and notice the gap after one thought has ended and before the next has arrived.

What it means for practice

No-mind does not mean destroying the mind. It means moving from being its slave to being its master. Use language when language is needed; when there is no need for communication, you can also be free of automatic verbalizing. Thoughts may still exist, but they no longer force you to follow them twenty-four hours a day.

Between Seeing and Naming
  1. Choose one simple object today: a tree, a glass of water, or a sound.
  2. For a few seconds, first see, hear, or feel without rushing to name or judge it in your head.
  3. If words have already appeared, do not stop them; simply notice that 'naming is happening' and keep sensing the spaces between words.
