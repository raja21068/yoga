The Mind Re-enters and Deceives

One of the mind's subtlest tricks is to label silence the moment it appears and announce, 'I have arrived.' In this way it enters again through another door.

Read the Book3. The Tricks of the Mind

What this chapter is saying

The book first warns that when thinking stops for a moment, if you instantly say, 'This is emptiness,' or 'Something great is happening,' thinking has already restarted. Silence appears, and the mind occupies it again through explanation and naming.

A deeper trick

The mind can also create dreams of 'I have become enlightened,' just as a sleeping person who is hungry can dream of a feast. When the longing of the seeker is strong, the mind can even produce experiences that match expectation and thereby protect the old sleep.

What it means for practice

When silence appears, do not rush to explain it. When unusual experiences arise, do not immediately build a new identity around them. Keep waiting and witnessing; let the fact unfold by itself. The point is not to doubt everything, but to prevent conclusions from running ahead of experience.

When Silence Appears, Say Nothing at First
  1. When thoughts such as 'I am very silent now' or 'I have understood' appear, see that they too are thoughts.
  2. Do not chase proof or denial; let the experience pass completely.
  3. Later, look back: has it made you clearer, simpler, and less attached to identity?
