Where Did You Leave Your Bicycle?

A fast-moving mind is not the enemy. Rather than pressing the accelerator and brake at the same time, watch where the mind runs and what is driving it.

Read the Book4. Where Did You Leave Your Bicycle?

What is the question?

The questioner says that in meditation the mind still moves at five hundred miles an hour, silence never lasts, and witnessing flashes only briefly, so he wonders whether he is wasting his time.

The story in the book

A priest thinks his bicycle has been stolen and plans to use the Ten Commandments to observe the suspects; when he reaches 'Thou shalt not commit adultery,' he suddenly remembers where he left the bicycle. The point is that the mind's movement usually has roots. Understanding where it runs is more useful than violently trying to stop it.

What it means for practice

Do not damage the intelligence of the mind in the name of silence. Let it move while you watch its direction, desires, and roots. Even very brief moments of witnessing are not wasted; the book asks you to be grateful for those flashes and let them gradually gather.

Do Not Press the Accelerator and Brake at the Same Time
  1. When the mind is fast, do not immediately order it to stop.
  2. Notice where it repeatedly goes: plans, sex, money, judgment, fear, arguments?
  3. Even if you see clearly for only a few seconds, count that as witnessing already happening—not as failure.
