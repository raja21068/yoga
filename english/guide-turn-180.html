Just a 180-Degree Turn

The changing experience of 'sometimes witnessing, sometimes not' is itself seen by something more continuous. The book compares the turn to facing a mirror that has always been reflecting you.

Read the Book5. Just a 180-Degree Turn

What is the question?

The questioner finds witnessing flickering like a turn signal: on, off, on, off. He wonders whether the road home will always be this way.

The turn in the answer

The book points out that if you can know 'now witnessing is here' and 'now it is not,' there is already a more continuous reflection behind those changes. What flickers may be a state; it is not necessarily the deepest witnessing.

The image in the book

Your being is like a mirror that has always reflected, but you have been facing away from it. No complicated invention is required—only a 180-degree turn back toward it, and then the gradual cleaning of dust accumulated over years.

Turn from the State Toward What Sees the State
  1. Today, do not ask only 'Am I witnessing or not?'
  2. Watch the changing 'yes / no' itself.
  3. Shift attention from the temporary state toward the continuity that knows the state has changed.
