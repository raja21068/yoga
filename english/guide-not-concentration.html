Meditation Is Not Concentration

Concentration narrows awareness to one point; meditation opens toward the whole. One creates tension, while the other begins in relaxation and acceptance.

Read the Book2. Wrong Methods

What this chapter is saying

The book clearly distinguishes concentration from meditation. Concentration is useful in scientific work because it excludes other things and gathers force at one point. But if the same contraction is mistaken for meditation, awareness becomes increasingly closed.

The contrast in the book

Concentration says, 'This is important; everything else is a distraction.' Meditation moves in the opposite direction. Birdsong, traffic, wind, bodily sensation—none must be rejected. Existence happens as a whole in the same moment; through relaxation and acceptance the meditator opens to the entire field.

What it means for practice

When sounds, bodily sensations, or thoughts appear, do not immediately label them distractions. If rejection creates tension, you have returned to concentration. Witnessing here is more like a wide space: whatever appears is allowed to be seen.

Open, Rather Than Narrow
  1. After sitting down, relax the body before choosing any narrow focus.
  2. Hear near and distant sounds, feel breath and body, without chasing any one of them.
  3. When you notice resistance to a sound, drop the judgment 'this should not be here' and return to the whole field of awareness.
