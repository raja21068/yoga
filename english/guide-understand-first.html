First Understand the Technique

Understand one method clearly before practicing it. Do not casually mix, modify, or 'improve' it according to personal preference.

Read the Book1. Methods and Meditation

What this chapter is saying

The book treats mind as an extremely subtle field and therefore warns against random experimentation when a method is not understood. Different methods may use very different, even opposite, approaches. A shared direction does not mean they can be mixed into a new technique.

The story in the book

The book tells a story of an old doctor improvising a solution that happens to work, then says clearly that meditation must not be practiced by this logic. 'When you don't know what to do, do something—anything' is not a good principle here.

What it means for practice

Understand the stages, timing, and sequence on the website before beginning. If a step is unclear, return to the instructions or video. Do not practice one method while inserting stages from another.

Check Before You Begin
  1. Do I know how many stages this method has?
  2. Do I know what to do in each stage, how long it lasts, and when it ends?
  3. If the answer is no, understand first and then begin.
