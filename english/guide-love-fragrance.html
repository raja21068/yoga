Love: The Fragrance of Meditation

The book sees love as the fragrance that naturally comes from meditation: not clinging to one person, but an inner quality that wants to share.

Read the Book2. Meditation in Flowering

What this chapter is saying

The book distinguishes love that comes from the mind from love that comes from meditation. The first often revolves around need, relationship, and demands for permanence; the second appears as thoughts lessen and inner space deepens, until love becomes a quality of being.

The image in the book

Meditation is compared to a lotus opening, and love is its fragrance. Fragrance is not reserved for one particular person; it comes from the flower itself. In the same way, the love described here is not only a relationship but a way of being.

What it means for practice

This is not a demand to 'love better,' nor a moral instruction to give more. The direction is the opposite: become clearer and quieter inside, then notice whether love slowly moves from needing a return toward natural sharing.

A Recognition
  1. When an impulse of love appears today, do not immediately judge whether it is high or low.
  2. Simply see what is present: need, exchange, fear of loss—or a sharing that is not in a hurry to take.
