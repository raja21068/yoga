Intelligence: The Ability to Respond

Here, 'intelligence' does not mean the amount of knowledge you possess, but the ability to respond freshly to the real situation in front of you.

Read the Book2. Meditation in Flowering

What this chapter is saying

The book understands intelligence as responsiveness: life is continually changing, and if you apply ready-made answers to new situations, you are not responding to the present—you are repeating the past.

The image in the book

Social conditioning is compared to rocks blocking a spring. Meditation does not add something new to the spring; it removes the obstruction so the intelligence already there can flow again.

What it means for practice

Meditation is therefore not another collection of 'right answers.' It is closer to clearing automatic reactions, habits, and borrowed conclusions so you can look at the present situation again with fresh eyes.

Try This Today
  1. In a moment that calls for an answer or decision, pause first.
  2. Notice whether the first thing that appears is an old answer or whether you have actually seen the present situation.
  3. There is no need to rebel against tradition on purpose; simply let the response come from now.
