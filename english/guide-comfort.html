Be Comfortable

The standard for posture is not whether it looks correct, but whether the body can gradually be forgotten.

Read the Book2. Advice for Beginners

What this chapter is saying

The book is not attached to sitting on the floor, crossing the legs, or using a chair. If the body keeps hurting, going numb, or feeling awkward, attention will continually be pulled back to it. Comfort is a basic condition for going deeper.

The test in the book

When are you comfortable? When you are no longer constantly thinking about the body. This test is more direct than imitating a particular posture.

What it means for practice

Comfort does not mean collapsing or falling asleep. It means being stable, sustainable, and able to breathe freely. This matters especially in passive meditation: let the body support the practice rather than become another battlefield.

Posture Check
  1. The spine need not be rigid; let breathing remain natural.
  2. If a chair suits you better than the floor, use a chair.
  3. Adjust until you can remain for a while without continuously fighting the body.
