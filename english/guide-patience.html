Patience

Keep the longing, drop the hurry. A seed needs care, but it cannot be pulled upward to make it grow faster.

Read the Book3. Guidelines to Freedom

What this chapter is saying

The book separates patience from indifference. Longing remains and practice continues; what drops is the demand that 'it must happen now.'

The metaphor in the book

Once a seed is planted, you can water it, care for it, and then sit in the shade and wait. Pulling the sprout upward does not make it grow faster. Meditation also has its own rhythm of ripening.

What it means for practice

Patience does not mean stopping practice. It means doing the work and then not forcing the result. Especially when one or two days seem to bring 'nothing,' do not immediately change methods or announce failure.

When You Become Impatient
  1. Keep the longing to go deeper.
  2. See clearly the demand 'why hasn't it happened yet?'
  3. Return to today's practice rather than fighting with a future result.
